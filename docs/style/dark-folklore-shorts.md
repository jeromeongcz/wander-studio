# Style guide: "Dark folklore shorts" (learned from 6 reference videos)

Purpose: creative guidance for original faceless horror/folklore shorts. **Apply the principles; never reuse the references' characters, scenes, scripts or story beats.** Keys: **[M]** measured from the files, **[S]** seen in frames, **[G]** educated guess (not provable from the files).

## 0. The references

All six: vertical ~9:16 (576×1024), 30 fps, 62–73 s, stereo AAC, burned-in word captions, platform end card on four of them. Labelled V1–V6 in upload order.

| | Subject (descriptive) | Shots (hard cuts) | Avg shot | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | Modern bedroom haunting; thin pale entity at a window | 19 | 3.3 s | Slowest cutting, most **acted** character animation; captions sit lower (67% of height); no red words |
| V2 | European folk-horror witch, house on legs | 34 | 1.9 s | Fastest, push-in heavy (16 of 32 shots); red name words; AI-generated label shown |
| V3 | Japanese roadside ghost-story, sunset palette | 40 | 1.9 s | Biggest push-ins (up to +25%); evidence inserts; **glitch scare ending** |
| V4 | Japanese street ghost-story, beauty-to-skull reveal | 34 | 2.1 s | Most lateral drift (19 of 33); split-face reveal at the end |

Common spine [S]: every one is **a story told by a continuous voice-over, illustrated one picture per sentence**, with one big word on screen at a time, a new camera-moving picture every ~2–3 s, a near-black palette lit by a single warm or cold source, and a final scare/cliffhanger.

## 1. Art direction and visual identity

- **Medium [S]:** 2D digital illustration, AI-generated look (V2 shows an "AI-generated" label; the rest share one engine/style) rendered like a **gritty 90s adult cartoon crossed with anime-horror**: thick dark contour lines, flat cel-style colour with soft painterly gradients, visible texture (wood grain, grass, brick, fabric). Not photoreal, not 3D, not flat vector.
- **Character design [S]:** caricatured and slightly gaunt. Large round eyes with small pupils (haunted stare); wide, lipless grimace mouths; long angular limbs on entities; wrinkled, hollow-cheeked faces; victims are plain, expressive, softer-featured (hoodie kid, schoolchildren, kimono-clad woman). Entities escalate by **distortion, not gore**: elongated body (V1 pale tall figure), skull half-face (V4 1:00–1:02), blank/smeared face (V1 0:25–0:27), glowing eyes in a black void (V1 0:07, V6 0:07).
- **Environments [S]:** lived-in, specific, readable at a glance: bedroom with nightlight and window blinds (V1 0:00), forest with a house on bird legs (V2 0:00), power-line village lane at dusk (V3), lantern-lit alley (V4), mountain village (V5), backstage corridors and theatre curtains (V6). Always **layered depth**: foreground silhouette, midground subject, background with a light source.
- **Consistency [S]:** same line weight, grain and colour logic across all shots. Each video also keeps its characters recognisable across 30+ shots (same hair, outfit, props), which is what makes a story of cut pictures cohere.

### Colour and light [M]
- Very dark: mean brightness 14–20% of full scale; 71–83% of pixels are "very dark"; mean saturation 39–47%.
- Dominant near-blacks are **blue-green blacks, not neutral black**: `#090B11`, `#0B110E`, `#0F1612`, `#111522`. Mid-tones are teal-slate `#1B323D`/`#2E4B5D`/`#293D49`, and olive-ochre `#2E3021`/`#3D3722`/`#5E4E34`.
- Rare accents carry the image (1–4% of pixels): lantern/candle amber `#B27934`, warm highlight `#AA9C66`, cold blue-white `#C9CCCF`/`#758999`.
- Colour temperature does the story's work [S]: **cold blue** = night, threat, screens (V1 TV glow 0:15–0:17); **amber** = refuge/lures/lanterns (V4, V3 0:47); **sunset orange gradient** = uneasy normality before the horror (V3 0:00–0:20); **sickly green** = monster presence (V1 0:57, V2 0:15 skulls).
- Light rules [S]: one practical source per shot (moon, lantern, candle, TV, spotlight); strong rim light and silhouette; **glowing eyes are the brightest thing in a dark frame**; faces are lit from the side or below at climaxes.
- Red is used for text emphasis, **#AC1B11 as sampled** (compression-darkened; design it as a saturated red ≈ `#D4181C`), and sparingly in scenes (kimono sash, blood).

## 2. Composition and framing rules [S]

1. **Vertical stack:** place the subject in the middle third; keep the caption row clear (it sits at 50% of height in V2–V6, 67% in V1), so nothing important lives behind the caption.
2. **Frames within frames:** windows, doorways, curtains, tree trunks and fence posts bracket the subject (V1 window 0:05, V2 trees, V6 curtains 0:47). Strong foreground silhouettes on one or both edges.
3. **Leading lines to a vanishing point:** lanes, corridors, floorboards, stage lights converge on the subject (V3 0:10, V4 0:00, V6 0:30).
4. **Symmetry for dread:** centred subject, mirrored architecture (V6 0:50, V5 0:52.5, V1 0:52.5).
5. **Scale tells power:** monsters fill the frame or loom from the top (V2 0:37.5 hands, V1 0:57.5), victims are small in wide shots.
6. **Angles:** low angle for threats, eye level for dialogue/reaction, over-the-shoulder or back-of-head for "someone is watching" (V6 0:55, V5 0:10).
7. **Insert shots** of evidence/objects: footprints (V3 0:50), scratch marks (V3 0:47), bandaged foot (V3 0:42.5), candle (V6 0:22.5), hair on the floor (V4 0:47.5). They give the eye a rest and give the story proof.
8. **Found-footage insert:** one desaturated CCTV-style frame with a timestamp overlay "03:17:42 AM" (V1 0:20–0:22.5), a different texture inside the same style.
9. **Diegetic text in the picture:** spoken lines rendered as small white text inside the scene near the speaker ("Let me in… I'm running out of time", V1 0:30–0:35; "please open the window…", V1 0:42.5).

## 3. Animation and motion

### 3.1 What the pictures actually do
- **A virtual camera is on almost every shot [M]:** of 29–37 measured shots per video, only 1–7 are near-static.
  - Push-in: typically **+5% to +10% scale over the shot** (average 5.2–10.4%; max +25% over 2.2 s, V3 0:47.3).
  - Pull-out: −4.5% to −9% (average), used for establishing/aftermath, dominant in V5.
  - Lateral drift: content moves up to **~10–17% of frame width** over 2–4 s (V4 0:40.4, V5 0:18.3), seldom vertical.
  - Speed is **near-linear** (mid-shot speed ÷ edge speed = 0.87–1.19): **no visible ease-in/ease-out**. Use linear or a barely-eased move.
  - Rates ~2–4% scale per second. Subtle on environments, stronger on faces and reveals.
- **Local animation inside the frame [S]**, after cancelling the camera: 9–17 of 22–31 shots per video have >3% of pixels moving independently. It is generated-clip motion, not hand animation: characters turn, step back, reach; a creature climbs in through a window while the boy retreats (V1 0:44.9–0:50); two figures turn toward each other (V5 0:25–0:29); spotlights pulse in sequence (V6 0:45.7–0:47.6); candle/lamp flicker; hair and fabric drift.
- **No classical animation principles [S]:** no squash and stretch, no obvious anticipation/follow-through, no walk cycles; motion is slow, floaty and **physically plausible but weightless**, which reads as uncanny. Loops: none observed.
- **Eyes and faces** hold still while the camera moves in: the stare is the animation.

### 3.2 Camera vocabulary and when to use it [M]/[S]
| Move | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Slow push-in (+5–10%) | tension, closing in on a face, a name, a threat | V2 0:19.4 (+9%), V4 0:01–0:04 (+18%), V3 0:47–0:51 (+25%) |
| Fast-ish push to a face | the entity reveal, about 5 s in | V2 0:05, V3 0:05, V4 0:05, V6 0:05 |
| Pull-out / reveal wide | establishing, aftermath, loneliness | V1 0:14.2 (−11%), V4 1:01 (−16%), V5 0:21.8 (−13%) |
| Lateral drift | walking victims, scanning a place | V4 0:40.4, V5 0:18.3 |
| Vertical drift | looming, climbing, hands from above | V3 vertical drift in 9 shots; V2 0:37.5 |
| Static/near-static | a held stare, a locked-off stage | V5 4 shots, V2 7 shots |
| Shake | not observed | none |

### 3.3 Glitch scare (finale) [S]
V3 (1:07) and V5 (1:05): the last face close-up **flickers with a ~0.6 s analog glitch**: RGB channel split (magenta/green fringes), horizontal scan-line displacement/smear, brightness flicker on/off about 3 times, caption removed. Use once per video as the final sting.

## 4. Transitions

- **Hard cuts only [M]:** 100% of shot changes are single-frame cuts (no dissolves, fades, wipes, zooms or flash-frames detected; videos start at full brightness on frame 0 and end without a fade).
- **Scale cut on the same subject [S]:** cut from a close framing to a wider framing of the same setting/character (V2 2.7 s: close house to wide house), then push in again. Keeps the eye in place while renewing the image.
- **Cut on the word:** a picture change usually coincides with a caption word change; every cut is aligned to the narration beat [S].
- **Impact on the cut [M]:** low-frequency "hits" accompany many cuts (V2 11 of 15 hits and V6 11 of 16 hits fall within 150 ms of a cut; V1 5/20, V3 9/17, V4 4/8, V5 8/21), so **hits are selective**: reserved for reveals and shocks, not every cut.
- **First cut at ~1.0–1.2 s** in V2–V6, then a steady 2–3 s rhythm.

## 5. Typography and motion graphics

- **Word-by-word captions [M]:** one word at a time, centred, **uppercase heavy italic sans**, white fill, dark outline with a soft drop shadow. Median word on screen **200–300 ms** (p10 ≈100–133 ms, p90 ≈370–500 ms) at about **3.0–3.6 words/s** (≈180–215 wpm).
- **Size [M]:** cap height ≈ 2.8–3.0% of frame height (≈ 55 px on 1920 tall; font size ≈ 75–80 px). Position: **vertical centre at 50%** (V2–V6), 67% in V1.
- **No pop/scale animation [S]:** the word appears instantly and is replaced instantly (frame-by-frame check, V2 0:01.5–0:02).
- **Red emphasis [S]/[M]:** the first mention of the entity's name or the key horror noun goes red (V2 "BABA" 0:05, "BLOOD" 0:30, "WHISPERING" 0:40; V4 "ONNA" 0:05; V5 "NAGORO" 0:02.5; V6 "PRINCE" 0:05). Red-emphasis frames are only 1–4% of each video.
- **Red hand-drawn arrow** in the first ≈3 s pointing at the subject (V3 0:02.5, V4/V5/V6 0:00–0:02.5): a hook device that directs the eye.
- **Platform overlays** (TikTok watermark, @handle, end-card with search bar, "AI-generated" label) are not style; omit or replace with own branding.

## 6. Editing, pacing and storytelling

- **One picture per sentence [S]:** the image literally illustrates the line being read. Shots: 1.9–2.2 s (fast) or 3.0–3.3 s (slow) → ~6–7 words per shot at fast pace.
- **Pace [M]:** 19–40 shots per ~65 s; continuous narration (silence only 1–11% of the time). Short pauses appear before the final scare.
- **Reveal timing [S]:** entity or name reveal close-up at **≈5 s** (V2, V3, V4, V6) or name-card at 2.5 s (V5).
- **Beat structure (derive your own) for ≈65 s:**
  1. 0–3 s: establishing picture, subject small, moody environment; first line is a promise or place ("There's a village…").
  2. 3–12 s: set-up, normal-looking victim; reveal close-up at ≈5 s.
  3. 12–35 s: rules/escalation in 8–12 shots, insert shots as proof.
  4. 35–55 s: confrontation, 1–2 big looming frames, strongest push-ins.
  5. 55–65 s: final reveal, **glitch scare or lone-silhouette wide**, last line unresolved.
- **Emotional engine:** dread through implication (insert shots, silhouettes, glowing eyes) more than showing; scares are 1–2 distorted faces per video.

## 7. Sound [M]

- Integrated loudness −21.7 to −31 LUFS (the two `.mov` exports are quietest at ≈−31). LRA 1.5–11.6 LU. For delivery target ≈ −14 LUFS normally; treat as a re-mix decision.
- Narration is continuous and steady, gaps 1–11%. [G] sounds like a synthetic or heavily processed narration voice; unverifiable.
- **Bed:** a low drone/ambient bed sits **~9–18 dB under the voice** (V1–V4, V6); V5 has **no bed**: dry voice with impacts only.
- **Impacts/booms:** 8–21 per video, low-frequency, mostly on reveals; share aligned with a cut: V2 73%, V6 69%, V3 53%, V5 38%, V4 50%, V1 25%.
- No audible silence-then-hit measured [not tested]; stingers on the final scare are [G].

## 8. Similarities, differences, and what combines well

**Shared (the style):** dark teal-black palette with one amber/cold accent source; cartoon-horror line art; 1 picture per sentence; hard cuts; slow linear camera on every shot; one centred uppercase italic word at a time with red keywords; continuous narration with a low bed and selective impacts; entity reveal ≈5 s; final scare.

**Differences to exploit as dials:**
- Cutting speed: V1/V5 ≈3 s (acting, atmosphere) ↔ V2/V3 ≈1.9 s (urgency).
- Camera bias: push-in heavy (V2, V3) ↔ pull-out/establishing heavy (V5).
- Motion: acted character motion (V1) ↔ near-still with camera (V4).
- Palette: blue (V1) ↔ sunset orange (V3) ↔ amber stage (V6).
- Caption position: lower-third 67% (V1) ↔ centre 50%.
- Ending: glitch scare (V3, V5) ↔ cliffhanger silhouette (V1, V6) ↔ abrupt close-up (V2).
- Bed: music under (most) ↔ dry (V5).

**Cohesive combination:** V4's palette discipline + V2's cut rate + V3's evidence inserts and glitch ending + V6's symmetry/light pulses + V1's found-footage insert and diegetic text, with V5-style dry silence before the final scare.

## 9. Implementation recipes

**Stills + camera (all engines):** render each shot from a high-res image with a *linear* scale/translate: scale 1.00→1.05–1.10 over 2–3 s; lateral drift 8–15% of width. Always crop the vertical window around a focal point.
- *Remotion:* `<Img>` with `interpolate(frame,[0,dur],[1,1.08],{easing:Easing.linear})` on `transform: scale() translateX()`; sequence shots with `<Sequence>` hard cuts; word captions from per-word timings, one `<span>` at a time.
- *CSS/JS:* `@keyframes push { to { transform: scale(1.08) } } animation: push 2.5s linear forwards`.
- *Three.js (2.5D):* image on a plane (or depth-sliced layers) and a perspective camera dolly of 2–4% over 2–3 s; for parallax, 3 layers with 2–6% relative travel.
- *FFmpeg:* `zoompan` on an oversampled still (already implemented in this project's render engine).
- *Local motion:* image-to-video model (optional, not bundled) for 2–4 s clips of 5–15% pixel motion; or cheap fakes: candle flicker via brightness noise, drifting fog layers, eye-glow pulse, hair sway via small mesh warp.

**Captions:** `font: 900 italic ~4% of frame height, system "Montserrat/Poppins Black Italic" [G], uppercase`, fill `#FFFFFF`, stroke 2–3 px `#000000`, shadow 0 2px 6px rgba(0,0,0,.6); one word, no animation; keyword colour `#D4181C`; hold each word for its spoken duration.

**Glitch scare:** 15–20 frames: duplicate frame in R/G/B, offset ±6–10 px horizontally in opposite directions; random horizontal slice displacement (8–40 px) on 10–20% of rows; alternate clean/glitched every 2–3 frames; remove caption; optional 3 frame darkening.

**Impact hit:** 80–200 ms sub-bass thump (40–90 Hz) with short noise burst, placed on the first frame of the new shot; duck the bed 6–8 dB for 400 ms.

**In this project's engine (Wander AI Director):**
- Already possible: hard cuts (`transition_in: cut`), linear-ish push/pan/Ken Burns per scene, caption position override, music bed with ducking, SFX cues at times, word captions (reduce `max_words` to 1 via a custom zone/style).
- Not yet supported (candidates for later): per-word emphasis colour, centred caption default, glitch effect, hand-drawn arrow overlay, in-picture diegetic text, found-footage timestamp overlay, end card, automatic "impact on cut" SFX, image-to-video clips, scale-cut generation.

## 10. Shot recipes (use as starting points)

| Shot | Frame | Camera | Length | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Establish | wide, subject small, source light visible | pull-out or lateral drift 8–12% | 2.5–4 s | bed only |
| Entity reveal (~5 s) | face close-up, glowing/huge eyes, name in red | push +8–12% | 1.5–2.5 s | impact on cut |
| Victim reaction | medium-close, wide eyes, rim light | push +4–6% | 1.5–2 s | bed |
| Looming threat | low angle, silhouette, hands/shape from top edge | slow tilt/push | 2–3 s | low swell |
| Evidence insert | object or floor detail with a light pool | push +5–8% | 1.5–2.5 s | none or tiny hit |
| Found footage | desaturated, timestamp overlay | static or tiny drift | 2–3 s | bed muted |
| Crowd lineup | symmetrical wide, rows of figures | pull-out | 3–4 s | bed |
| Final scare | face close-up + glitch flicker | static/push | 0.8–1.5 s | impact, then silence |

## 11. Originality rules
- Invent your own characters, creatures, locations, names and stories. Do not reproduce any reference character design, scene composition, script text, or channel branding.
- Reuse only the **grammar**: palette logic, camera/cut rhythm, caption behaviour, sound recipe, beat structure.
- If a story draws on public folklore, retell it in new words and new imagery.

## 12. Pre-flight checklist
1. Single practical light source per shot? Eyes or the key object brightest?
2. Every shot has a linear camera move (or a reason to be still)?
3. One picture per sentence; first cut ≈1 s; shots 2–3 s?
4. Entity/name reveal near 5 s, name in red caption?
5. Captions: one word, uppercase italic, no animation, centred; nothing important behind them?
6. Bed 9–18 dB under voice; impacts only on reveals; silence before the final scare?
7. Finale: glitch scare or silhouette cliffhanger, last line unresolved?
8. Original characters and story; no platform overlays; licenses recorded for any asset.
